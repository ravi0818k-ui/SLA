TUTORIALS vs THE INTERVIEW

You watched it all. So why can't you explain it?

Because watching and recalling are two different things. See the difference:

Watching tutorialsIn the interview
Pause, rewind – the answer is on the screenNo screen – you explain it from memory
Watched today, feels clear todayAsked weeks later, after twenty other topics
The same example as the videoA new scenario, in new words
Follow along, step by stepSolve it yourself, with someone watching
Comfortable, at homeNerves, a clock and one chance

Tutorials make a topic feel familiar; interviews ask you to recall and apply it. That's a separate skill – and it can be trained.

THE REAL REASON

It's not your ability – it's the method

More hours and more courses won't fix this. A better way of learning will – and you can use it on any subject.

Familiar isn't the same as known

Re-watching makes a topic feel easy. The interview needs you to pull the answer out of your own head.

Forgetting is normal – plan for it

Without short revisions at the right gaps, much of what you studied last week fades before the interview.

Cramming breaks under new questions

Memorised answers need the same words. Understanding plus memory hooks survives a twisted question.

Stress blocks recall

Under pressure, even what you know won't come out. Staying calm is a skill you can practise.

AFTER THE WORKSHOP

A system you can use on any new skill

  • Close the tab and recall: after each video or chapter, write down what you remember before moving on (active recall).
  • A revision schedule for interview topics: short reviews at growing gaps instead of re-watching everything (spaced repetition).
  • Explain it like you're in the interview: the Feynman technique turns every topic into interview practice.
  • Memory hooks: for terms, commands, frameworks, formulas and numbers.
  • Read faster, focus longer: skim and scan long docs, and use focus sprints to beat job-hunt distraction.
